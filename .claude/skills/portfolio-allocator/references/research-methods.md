# Research Methods Reference

Use this when running Phase 3 (status quo) and Phase 4 (candidate generation). Pick the relevant subsection.

## Micro-cap research

Micro-caps need extra scrutiny because data is thin and manipulation is real.

**Always check**:
- Cash runway: does the company have enough cash to execute the thesis without dilution?
- Recent S-1, S-3, F-1, or equivalent filings — these signal upcoming dilution
- Insider transactions (Form 4 in US, equivalents elsewhere) — heavy insider selling is a flag
- Going concern language in latest financials
- Auditor changes (often a red flag if recent)
- Short interest and days-to-cover

**Search patterns that work**:
- `[ticker] dilution`
- `[ticker] going concern`
- `[ticker] insider selling`
- `[company name] earnings [latest quarter]`
- `[ticker] short interest`
- `[company name] [primary product/tech] competitor`

**Asymmetric risk/reward checklist**:
- Downside cap: real revenue OR meaningful cash on balance sheet OR strategic value to potential acquirer
- Upside path: TAM credibility, moat formation, real product-market fit signals (not just press releases)
- Time-to-thesis: can this play out in 5-10 years? If shorter, it might be a trade not an investment

## Large-cap research

For ISA large-caps, depth matters less per name but quality bar is higher.

**Always check**:
- Forward P/E vs. historical and vs. peer median
- Free cash flow conversion
- Capital allocation: buybacks, dividends, M&A history
- Moat durability: pricing power, customer concentration, regulatory exposure
- Recent analyst price target distribution (look at the spread, not just consensus)

**Search patterns**:
- `[ticker] forward P/E`
- `[ticker] free cash flow [year]`
- `[company name] competitive moat analysis`
- `[ticker] analyst price target`

## ETF research

ETFs have specific evaluation criteria.

**Always check**:
- Expense ratio (lower is generally better but not the only factor)
- AUM (avoid sub-£100M funds for liquidity reasons)
- Tracking error vs. stated benchmark
- Replication method: physical vs. synthetic (synthetic carries counterparty risk)
- Domicile: Ireland-domiciled ETFs are typically more tax-efficient for UK ISA holders than US-domiciled
- Distribution: accumulating vs. distributing (ISA investors often prefer accumulating for compounding)
- Underlying index methodology: how often rebalanced, what weighting scheme, sector caps

**Search patterns**:
- `[ETF ticker] expense ratio`
- `[ETF ticker] tracking error`
- `[ETF ticker] vs [alternative] comparison`
- `[index name] methodology`

**For Revolut specifically**: Revolut's ETF universe is more limited than a full broker. Verify availability before recommending. UCITS (Ireland-domiciled) ETFs are the typical universe for UK ISA investors.

## Entry-point evaluation

Use this rubric to tag candidates.

**Undervalued entry**:
- Trading meaningfully below fair value estimates (analyst median, DCF, or peer multiples)
- Recent drawdown not driven by thesis-breaking news
- Risk/reward skewed favorably at current price

**Fair entry, high conviction**:
- Trading near fair value
- Thesis is strong enough that paying fair price is acceptable
- Quality of business reduces the need for valuation discount

**Premium but exceptional**:
- Trading above typical valuation but justified by business quality
- Use sparingly — only for businesses where the moat is so strong that paying up is rational
- Should be rare in the recommendation list

## Sources to prioritize

Generally reliable for English-language equity research:
- Company filings (10-K, 10-Q, 20-F, annual report)
- Reuters, Bloomberg, FT for news
- Seeking Alpha for thesis depth (treat with skepticism — it's opinion)
- Yahoo Finance, Google Finance for quick price/multiple checks
- Morningstar for ETF analysis
- justETF for UCITS ETF data (UK-relevant)

Sources to be skeptical of:
- Stock promotion newsletters
- Reddit/forum hype (occasionally surfaces genuine ideas, but verify independently)
- Press releases without independent corroboration

## When data is thin

For micro-caps especially, you'll hit cases where current data is sparse. In those cases:
- Say so plainly in the rationale
- Lower conviction tag
- Suggest the user verify before executing
- Do not invent data to fill gaps
