# Research Methods Reference

Use this in Phase 4 (market and calendar scan), Phase 6 (holding review) and Phase 8 (candidate screening). Pick the relevant subsection.

## Evidence standards (all research)

- **Date every figure** and note its source. Prices and multiples go stale within days.
- **Two sources** for anything that drives a verdict (an earnings figure, a dilution event, an analyst target). If sources disagree, say so and use the more primary one (filings beat articles).
- **Primary over secondary:** company filings, earnings releases and transcripts first; news second; opinion last.
- **Separate fact from interpretation** in working notes so the report can state which is which.
- **When data is thin, say so**, lower conviction, and list it under evidence gaps. Never invent figures.

## Materiality tiers for the holding review

- **Tier 1 (deep review):** positions over 3% of their portfolio; anything that moved more than 20% since last run; anything with an event in the next 45 days; anything near a kill criterion; every candidate for top-up or exit. Deep review = latest quarter's numbers, guidance, filings since last run, insider activity, analyst changes, thesis check against the register.
- **Tier 2 (sweep):** news, filings and earnings-date check. Promote to Tier 1 if anything material turns up.

## Micro-cap research

Micro-caps need extra scrutiny because data is thin and manipulation is real. Small caps as a group only earn their premium once low-quality "junk" is screened out (Asness et al., "Size Matters, if You Control Your Junk"), so the quality gates below matter.

**Screen for new shots (gates; a fail needs an explicit, written exception):**
- Market cap within the profile's band at entry (default under ~$2-3B; above ~$50M)
- Listed on a major exchange (NYSE, Nasdaq, LSE main/AIM, or an equivalent main market); no OTC-only names
- Liquidity: average daily traded value at least ~$1M, and a bid-ask spread that won't eat more than ~1% on entry
- Cash runway of at least ~6 quarters at the current burn, or profitable / cash-flow positive
- Share count growth under ~15% a year over the last 2-3 years, and no shelf offering likely to be used imminently, unless the raise funds a clearly value-creating milestone
- No going-concern language, auditor resignation or late filings
- Real product or real revenue, or for pre-revenue biotech, clinical data plus cash through the next readout
- Insider behaviour: open-market buying is a plus; heavy selling with no buying is a flag to explain
- Not a promotion: no paid newsletters pumping it, no sudden volume spike with no news

**Always check:**
- Cash runway and debt maturities
- Recent S-1, S-3, F-1, ATM programmes or equivalents (signal upcoming dilution); reverse splits; Nasdaq minimum-bid notices
- Insider transactions (Form 4 in the US, equivalents elsewhere)
- Going-concern language in latest financials; auditor changes
- Short interest and days-to-cover
- Customer concentration and the next binary event (trial readout, contract award, approval)

**Search patterns that work:**
- `[ticker] dilution` / `[ticker] offering` / `[ticker] ATM`
- `[ticker] going concern`
- `[ticker] insider buying` / `[ticker] insider selling`
- `[company name] earnings [latest quarter]`
- `[ticker] short interest`
- `[company name] [primary product/tech] competitor`

**Asymmetric risk/reward checklist:**
- Downside cap: real revenue OR meaningful cash on balance sheet OR strategic value to an acquirer
- Upside path: TAM credibility, moat formation, product-market fit signals (customers, retention, backlog), not just press releases
- Time-to-thesis: can this play out in 5-10 years? If shorter, it might be a trade, not an investment
- What 10x requires: revenue and margin needed at a reasonable multiple, and whether that is plausible

## Large-cap research

For ISA large-caps, depth per name matters less but the quality bar is higher.

**Always check:**
- Forward P/E (and EV/EBIT or FCF yield) vs. own history and peer median
- Free cash flow conversion and return on invested capital
- Balance sheet: net debt / EBITDA, maturities
- Capital allocation: buybacks, dividends, M&A record
- Moat durability: pricing power, customer concentration, regulatory exposure
- Analyst price target distribution (the spread, not just consensus) and recent revisions to earnings estimates
- How much of it the user already owns through the core ETFs (look-through)

**Search patterns:**
- `[ticker] forward P/E`
- `[ticker] free cash flow [year]`
- `[company name] competitive moat analysis`
- `[ticker] analyst price target`
- `[ticker] estimate revisions`

## ETF research

**Always check:**
- Ongoing charge (TER); lower is better but not the only factor
- Fund size (avoid sub-£100M funds: closure and liquidity risk)
- Tracking difference vs. its benchmark over several years
- Replication: physical vs. synthetic (synthetic carries counterparty risk)
- Domicile: Ireland-domiciled UCITS is the usual choice for UK investors (15% US dividend withholding at fund level)
- Accumulating vs. distributing (accumulating suits ISA compounding)
- Index methodology: weighting, caps, rebalance frequency, what's actually inside
- **Look-through overlap** with existing ETFs and direct holdings (top 10 holdings and sector weights from the factsheet)
- Trading line: a GBP-traded line avoids FX fees where the platform charges them; currency of the line is not the currency exposure
- For bond ETFs: duration, credit quality, and currency hedging (hold GBP or GBP-hedged for ballast)

**Thematic ETFs:** Morningstar data (via Capital Group) shows fewer than one in ten thematic funds survived and beat a global equity index over the 15 years to mid-2024, partly due to higher fees and buying themes after they have run. Require: a theme with a multi-year earnings case (not just a narrative), reasonable valuation vs. the broad market, a fund that holds the actual beneficiaries, and room under the thematic cap.

**Search patterns:**
- `[ETF ticker] ongoing charge` / `[ETF name] justETF`
- `[ETF ticker] tracking difference`
- `[ETF ticker] vs [alternative] comparison`
- `[index name] methodology`
- `[ETF name] top holdings`

**Broker check:** each broker's ETF universe is limited and its ISA list may differ from its general account. Verify the specific line is available inside the user's ISA on their ISA broker.

## Market and calendar scan sources

- Central bank calendars: Federal Reserve (FOMC), Bank of England (MPC)
- Economic calendar for CPI, payrolls, GDP
- Earnings dates: company investor relations pages, Nasdaq or Yahoo Finance earnings calendars
- Biotech catalysts: company releases, FDA PDUFA calendars
- Lock-up expiries and index rebalances (Russell reconstitution in June; S&P quarterly)

## Red team (finalists)

For each finalist, before conviction is stated:
1. **Bear case:** the strongest argument against, as a short seller would write it. Find a real bear source if one exists.
2. **Pre-mortem:** "It is two years from now and this is down 60%. What happened?" List the 2-3 most likely causes.
3. **Kill criteria:** the observable facts that would mean the thesis is broken (e.g. "cash runway under 4 quarters without a partner", "two consecutive quarters of declining orders"). These go in the thesis register.
4. **Answer or drop:** if the bear case can't be answered with evidence, drop the candidate.

## Entry-point evaluation

Tag every candidate and top-up.

**Undervalued:**
- Meaningfully below fair value (analyst median, DCF, or peer multiples)
- Recent drawdown not driven by thesis-breaking news
- Risk/reward skewed favourably at the current price

**Fair / Fair, high conviction:**
- Near fair value
- Thesis strong enough that paying a fair price is acceptable

**Premium but exceptional:**
- Above typical valuation but justified by business quality
- Rare; the scorecard should show whether these work for this user

**Top-ups use the same rubric.** Don't top up immediately after a sharp run.

## Sources to prioritise

The full source map, reliability tiers and access workarounds are in `data-sources.md`. In short, reliable for English-language equity research:
- Company filings (10-K, 10-Q, 20-F, 8-K, annual reports), SEC EDGAR, Companies House / RNS for UK
- Reuters, Bloomberg, FT for news
- Insider data: SEC Form 4 filings (EDGAR or aggregators)
- Seeking Alpha for thesis depth (treat as opinion)
- Yahoo Finance, Google Finance, stockanalysis.com for quick price, multiple and share-count checks
- Morningstar for fund analysis; justETF for UCITS ETF data
- Issuer factsheets (iShares, Vanguard, SPDR, Invesco, etc.) for holdings and charges

Be sceptical of:
- Stock promotion newsletters
- Reddit/forum hype (occasionally surfaces genuine ideas; verify independently)
- Press releases without independent corroboration
- Price targets without a stated method
